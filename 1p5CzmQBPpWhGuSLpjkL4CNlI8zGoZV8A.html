Snapshots From the Street

Moments of grace from this month.

An ordinary serve night… and an eternal moment

Wednesday night began like so many of our serve nights do...
Meals were shared. Showers were provided. Conversations unfolded. People gathered in community.

And then, in the middle of an ordinary night, something eternal happened.

Daniel believed in Jesus and chose to be baptized.

We may not know every chapter of Daniel’s story, but we know this moment marked the beginning of something new.

Baptism is a public declaration that says: I believe. I belong to Jesus. My old life has been buried. I am walking forward in new life.

This is why we keep showing up in the streets. We want to meet physical needs with dignity and love, but our greatest hope is that every person we encounter would come to know the hope, freedom, and new life found in Jesus.

No stage. No church building. No perfect circumstances required.

Just Jesus meeting Daniel right where he was and a community surrounding him as he said yes.

Daniel, your Salty Farm family celebrates with you. This is only the beginning.

Love moves in every direction here

The only card she received when her father passed away came from our friends on the street.

They saw that one of our volunteers was hurting. They wanted her to know she was loved, remembered, and not carrying her grief alone.

So they gave her a card.

To someone else, it may have seemed like a small gesture. To her, it meant everything.

Recently, another volunteer began transitioning through an incredibly difficult season and needed help moving. Once again, her Salty Farm family showed up...our team coming together to carry boxes, lighten the load, and remind her that she didn’t have to walk through this chapter alone.

This is what community looks like.

It isn’t divided into those who serve and those who are served. Love moves in every direction here.

Our volunteers show up for our friends.
Our friends show up for our volunteers.
And together, we carry one another through celebrations, losses, transitions, and everything in between.

Purpose isn’t always found in a title, a platform, or a program.

Sometimes it is found in noticing that someone is hurting and then...writing the card, carrying the box, showing up...reminding another person, “You matter to me.”

Somewhere along the way, we became more than people who gather to serve.

We became family. The Salty Farm Family.

And this is the kind of community we believe God is calling us to keep building...one where everyone belongs, everyone contributes, and everyone has a place to both give and receive love.
One table. One shared purpose.

There’s something special about a meal made by many hands.

Each week, our meals often come together potluck style...one person brings a main dish, another makes a side, someone brings dessert, another adds the sandwiches, fruit, and drinks. Different kitchens. Different recipes. Different people.

But one table.

And one shared purpose: to make sure our friends on the street are fed well and know they are deeply cared for.

We don't want to simply hand someone something to eat. We want to serve the kind of meal we would put on the table for our own family...hearty, healthy, flavorful, and made with intention.

Because food can meet a physical need, but the heart behind it can communicate so much more:

We thought about you.
We prepared this for you.
You are worth the effort.
There is a place for you at this table.

Every casserole stirred, vegetable chopped, dish seasoned, dessert baked, and plate served becomes part of something bigger when we bring it all together.

Maybe that's the secret ingredient in our meals....

A whole lot of people contributing what they can, all seasoned with love.

This is how community feeds community.

Meet needs. Restore dignity. Bring hope. Empower purpose. Cultivate community.
A Story From This Month
When “I’m ready” meets real-world barriers

“They are going to want things I don’t have...and it will end up like it always does. I can’t get help anywhere.”

Those were his words when I started trying to help him last night. He has battled alcoholism for 34 years. And last night, he was ready to live a different life. I was determined that this time would be different.

But he was right.

He thought he had insurance, but he didn’t know his member ID number. As we started making calls and trying to figure it out, we learned his coverage had been dropped after he failed to file his taxes.

And just like that...the place we were trying to get him into couldn’t take him.

As I sat beside him making calls, asking questions, searching for another way...I watched his frustration grow.

Then the hopelessness. Then the anger. Not because he didn’t want help. Because even when he was finally ready to do the next right thing...he couldn’t figure out how to get through all the barriers standing between him and that help.

This is the real side of ministry that doesn't always make it into a post. Sometimes it doesn't work. Sometimes there isn't a beautiful ending to the story we get to share the next morning. Sometimes someone says, “I'm ready”...and despite every phone call, every connection and every effort, we still can't get them where they need to go.

And then we all leave feeling helpless.

He left still needing help...and I left knowing that all my determination wasn't enough to change that.

Those nights hurt.

But they also teach us where the gaps are. Last night showed us a gap we need to get better at navigating...so we're going to.

We are putting together a resource training night with our best resource connection, Jacob Gilbertson with Banyan Treatment Center for our SFM volunteers...helping our team better understand insurance, treatment options, eligibility and how to navigate the resources available in our community.

Because I don't want all of that knowledge living with one person. I want more of our team equipped to sit beside someone in that moment and help them find the next right door.

Resources aren't always the problem. Connection to them is.

And sometimes there are barriers between “I'm ready” and “here's your next step” that most of us never have to think about.

We can't promise that every story will end the way we desperately want it to. But we can learn. We can become better equipped. We can keep showing up.

And when someone says, “I'm ready”...we can do everything possible to make sure they don't have to navigate what comes next alone.
